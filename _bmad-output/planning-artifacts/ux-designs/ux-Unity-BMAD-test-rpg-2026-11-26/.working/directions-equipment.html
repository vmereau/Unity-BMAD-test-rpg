Equipment section — three layout directions

Each frame is the real 1920×1080 Inventory screen shown at 50%. The item grid and the detail panel stay where they are and are dimmed here. Only the left area changes: it is 576 px wide (x 0–576) and the panels are 800 px tall (y 140–940), while today's equipment column uses only 200 px of that width. The seven slots come from EquipmentSlot: Weapon, Offhand, Helmet, Armor, Necklace, Ring ×2. All three frames show the same states: an occupied slot, an empty slot (Necklace, Ring 2), a hovered slot (warm edge) and the selected slot (brass), which feeds the detail panel. The icons are your real item icons.

APaper doll: a painted figure with slots placed on the body

This is the classic RPG layout. A flat 2D figure, a painted parchment silhouette of the hero as one illustration, sits in the middle of a 496×800 panel. Each slot sits next to the body part it covers, joined by a thin ink line: helmet at the head, necklace at the throat, armor at the chest, weapon and offhand at the hands, and the rings in a pair under the hands. The figure never changes with your gear, so it costs one illustration and no 3D work. It reads well, but the space is fixed: a new slot (boots, belt) needs a new spot on the body. A summary row at the bottom totals protection and damage. Its content depends on the stats your items actually expose.

BLive character: a 3D preview of the hero between two slot columns

The real player model is rendered to a texture between two columns of slots and wears exactly what is equipped. Equip the round shield and it shows on his arm. You can drag to turn him. Armor goes on the left (Helmet, Armor, Necklace) and arms and rings on the right (Weapon, Offhand, Ring, Ring). This is the most rewarding option: gear you find is seen on the hero, which fits a game where armor is physical and worn. It is also the most work: a preview camera, a light rig and a copy of the player that follows equip changes. The model has to look good up close, and an empty slot still needs the column to explain it.

CArmoury ledger: grouped rows with names and stats, no figure

There is no body. The panel is a ledger sorted into three engraved groups: Arms (Weapon, Offhand), Armour (Helmet, Armor) and Trinkets (Necklace, Ring, Ring). Each row puts the slot next to the item's name and its key numbers, so you compare gear without hovering. An empty row says so in words. This is the most sober and functional option, closest to Gothic's restraint and the ART_DIRECTION no UI clutter rule. It is cheap to build (it reuses SlotWell and Panel) and grows easily (a new slot is a new row). The trade-off is that it is the least "RPG character sheet" in feel: you read your gear more than you see it.
